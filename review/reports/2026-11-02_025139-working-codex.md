## Findings

### 1. Agreement with another transcription is used to infer recovery of true matches

`middle-initial-agreement-treated-as-true-match-evidence` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:547-552, replication/diagnostics/validation_fixed_initials.do:74-79`

**What the code does**  
The diagnostic defines validation as literal initial agreement: `gen byte vb_`src' = mi1930_old==mi_`src'` and `gen byte va_`src' = mi1930_new==mi_`src'`. Here `mi_src` is extracted from an Ancestry or FamilySearch transcription, not a verified identity or hand-labeled ground truth. The paper nevertheless calls this link “quality” and says the result suggests the model is “recovering true matches rather than creating spurious ones.”

**Why it matters**  
Two noisy transcriptions can agree on an incorrect initial, and disagreement can reflect transcription rather than linkage error. The reported agreement rates therefore do not identify the false-match rate or establish that the added links are true; this overstates what the new decomposition demonstrates.

**How to check**  
For added and dropped links, merge the link pairs to the independent genealogical reference standard used later in the paper and tabulate verified, contradicted, and unverifiable outcomes by `status`. Compare those results with `tab status va_anc` and `tab status va_fs` to show whether middle-initial agreement predicts independently verified links.

**Suggested fix**  
Describe these results strictly as middle-initial agreement, not accuracy or proof of true matching. Base the true-versus-spurious claim on the independent genealogical validation, ideally reporting its added-link and dropped-link results directly in this paragraph.

### 2. The added-versus-dropped comparison omits roughly 80 percent of links without disclosing the resulting selection

`added-link-validation-covers-only-one-fifth` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:551-552, replication/diagnostics/validation_fixed_initials.do:95-105, replication/results/validation_fixed_initials.tex:44-49, replication/results/validation_fixed_initials.tex:84-89`

**What the code does**  
For each status, the code first counts all links as `nl`, but `summarize `3'_`src'` computes the reported agreement mean only where the validation variable is nonmissing. The generated results show initial coverage of only 20.5% and 19.2% for Ancestry added and dropped links, and 20.8% and 18.6% for FamilySearch. The paper reports 82–85% versus 71% “of cases” and immediately generalizes to the quality of the new links.

**Why it matters**  
About four-fifths of added and dropped links cannot enter this validation comparison. Availability of a middle initial is plausibly selective by naming practice, nativity, transcription quality, and record legibility, so agreement among the observed fifth need not represent all added links—the population underlying the true-match claim.

**How to check**  
Run `tab status if congruent==0` and, for each source, `tab status if congruent==0 & !missing(va_anc)` or the corresponding before/FS variable; then compare race, nativity, literacy, name commonness, and legibility between links with and without usable initials.

**Suggested fix**  
State the approximately 20% coverage next to the 82–85% and 71% rates, label the comparison as conditional on usable initials, and add balance or independent-validation evidence showing whether evaluated and unevaluated links differ.
