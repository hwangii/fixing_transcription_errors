## Findings

### 1. The slide table labels middle-initial agreement as validation without ground truth

`middle-initial-agreement-labeled-validation` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:98, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:105, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:175, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:186-188`

**What the code does**  
The reported statistic is generated as `mi1930==mi1940` or `mianc1930==mianc1940`, conditional on nonempty initials and `linked_`c'==1`. The new slide column is then placed under rows labeled `Share validated`, although the expression measures cross-census initial agreement rather than whether the link or transcription is correct.

**Why it matters**  
The slide can be read as evidence that the mixed FamilySearch/ML links are accurate. With no hand-validated truth sample, agreement of noisy initials is only a validation proxy and can be high for an incorrect link or low for a correct link.

**How to check**  
Open the generated `MATCHING/results/fs_avg_performance_slides.tex` and trace its `Share validated` cells back to `val_fs30ml` and `valanc_fs30ml`; both are defined solely by the equality expressions at lines 98 and 105.

**Suggested fix**  
Relabel the rows as “middle-initial agreement among links” and state explicitly that this is a proxy, not accuracy. Reserve “validated” or “correct” for a hand-reviewed truth sample with a documented protocol.

### 2. Each method’s agreement rate conditions on a different, endogenously selected set of links

`method-specific-validation-denominators` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:96-105, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:122-127`

**What the code does**  
For each method, `linked_`c' = _merge==3 & fiveyr_band==1`; `val_`c'` and `valanc_`c'` are defined only when that method linked the record, and `summarize` computes separate means over those nonmissing values. Thus the displayed agreement rates compare different record populations rather than holding the linked cohort fixed.

**Why it matters**  
Changing the name source changes which records link, and transcription difficulty affects linkage. A higher mixed-method agreement rate can therefore arise because difficult records disappear or easier records enter, not because the resulting links are better. Fixed Ancestry initials remove one measurement-source difference but do not remove this selection difference.

**How to check**  
After the loop, tabulate `linked_ans linked_true linked_fs30ml if target==1`, then calculate `mean valanc_*` on (a) records linked by all three methods and (b) added, dropped, and switched-link groups separately. Compare these with the existing method-specific means.

**Suggested fix**  
Keep linkage rates on each method’s full target population, but supplement agreement rates with a common-link comparison and an added/dropped/switched-link decomposition. Describe the unrestricted rates as method-specific selected-sample statistics.

### 3. The new slide column is labeled FamilySearch/ML although both sources fall back to Ancestry field by field

`hybrid-sources-presented-as-pure-sources` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:175, MATCHING/1b_fs_divided_files.do:83-87, MATCHING/1_divide_and_clean_everyoneV5_women_addedV2.do:168-170`

**What the code does**  
The slide header says `FS 1930, ML 1940`. In fact, the FamilySearch file replaces `f_name` and `l_name` only when the respective cleaned FamilySearch field is nonempty, while the ML construction replaces missing ML first and last names with Ancestry values. The column therefore evaluates two hybrid transcriptions, not exclusively FamilySearch names in 1930 and ML names in 1940.

**Why it matters**  
Without the fallback caveat on the rendered slide, differences can be attributed to FamilySearch or ML even though an unknown share of fields remains Ancestry. This can materially change the interpretation of the comparison, especially if source coverage is selective.

**How to check**  
For the mixed link file, tabulate `fs_used1930` among five-year-band links. Separately merge the 1940 `_true_cleaned` and `_ans_cleaned` divided files by `id` and count records where each ML field equals its Ancestry fallback or where the original ML field was unavailable.

**Suggested fix**  
Label the column “FS where available 1930, ML where available 1940; otherwise Ancestry,” and report the field-level source shares for both years next to the result.
