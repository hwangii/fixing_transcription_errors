## Findings

### 1. The slide table labels middle-initial agreement as validation without ground truth

`middle-initial-agreement-labeled-validation` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:98, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:105, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:175, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:186-189`

**What the code does**  
Computes cross-census middle initial equality via `mi1930==mi1940` (and `mianc1930==mianc1940`) conditional on both initials being non-empty and `linked_`c'==1`. In the output LaTeX table for slides (`fs_avg_performance_slides.tex`), these rows are labeled `Share validated (%)` and `Share validated, Ancestry initials (%)`.

**Why it matters**  
Middle initials are noisy, non-unique, and subject to enumeration and transcription errors. Agreement across censuses is a consistency proxy, not ground truth validation; labeling it as validation in presentation slides violates the project standard that agreement is not accuracy.

**How to check**  
Inspect MATCHING/results/fs_avg_performance_slides.tex and trace the Share validated rows back to val_fs30ml and valanc_fs30ml in MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:98, 105.

**Suggested fix**  
Relabel table rows as 'Middle-initial agreement among links (%)' and add a table note stating that this measures cross-census consistency, not ground-truth link accuracy.

### 2. Validation rates condition on method-specific, endogenously selected link samples

`method-specific-validation-denominators` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:96-105, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:120-127`

**What the code does**  
Generates `val_`c'' and `valanc_`c'' only when `linked_`c'==1`, and computes means using `summarize` over non-missing values. As a result, each column's validation share is evaluated on a different, method-selected subsample of individuals.

**Why it matters**  
Changing transcription sources directly alters which records link. Hard-to-read names with lower literacy or atypical spelling link at different rates; differences in initial agreement across columns may reflect compositional selection of linked records rather than improved linkage quality.

**How to check**  
Compute agreement rates restricted to the intersection of records linked by all methods (linked_ans==1 & linked_true==1 & linked_fs30ml==1), and compare them to the method-specific averages currently reported.

**Suggested fix**  
Report validation rates on a common sample of links matched across all candidate methods, alongside a decomposition of added, dropped, and switched links.

### 3. Slide column header 'FS 1930, ML 1940' conceals field-by-field Ancestry fallbacks

`hybrid-sources-presented-as-pure-sources` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:175, MATCHING/1b_fs_divided_files.do:83-87, MATCHING/1_divide_and_clean_everyoneV5_women_addedV2.do:168-170`

**What the code does**  
Labels the new column `FS 1930, ML 1940` without indicating that FamilySearch replaces Ancestry only when non-empty in 1930, and ML replaces Ancestry only when non-empty in 1940, while reporting `0.0` for `Links with an FS name in 1940 (%)` and omitting the ML utilization share.

**Why it matters**  
The presentation table suggests a clean comparison between FamilySearch and ML models, but both sources retain substantial and potentially selective shares of Ancestry transcription baseline.

**How to check**  
Count records in link1930_1940_fs1930_ml1940_cleaned_noraceblock0.dta where 1930 names match Ancestry vs. FamilySearch, and where 1940 names match Ancestry vs. ML.

**Suggested fix**  
Clarify in the column label or slide note that transcriptions are hybrid (e.g. 'FS 1930 (hybrid), ML 1940 (hybrid)'), and report the proportion of linked records utilizing non-Ancestry transcriptions in both years.
