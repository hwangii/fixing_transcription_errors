## Findings

### 1. FamilySearch profile sample is treated as ground truth and labeled 'Verified Correct'

`selected-reference-labeled-verified-correct` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **likely** (implied, depends on unseen data)

**Where:** `MATCHING/score_fs_links.do:46-71, MATCHING/score_fs_links.do:217-220, MATCHING/score_fs_links.do:376, MATCHING/score_fs_links.do:392, CODE/create_linked_sample_for_MLP_training.do:5-15`

**What the code does**  
The updated constants in MATCHING/score_fs_links.do score proposed census links against 'A'S COMPLETE TRUTH FILE' (lines 46-47), counting links as verified where person_merge == 3 & histid1930 == true_histid1930 (line 217) and writing 'Verified Correct' into the LaTeX results table (line 392). However, CODE/create_linked_sample_for_MLP_training.do:5-15 shows this benchmark is constructed by inner-merging 1930 and 1940 FamilySearch-profile crosswalks and retaining only profiles with complete, ordered vital dates (birthyr, deathyr, birthmon, deathmon). The code treats agreement with a selected genealogical sample as ground truth.

**Why it matters**  
Individuals linked across censuses on FamilySearch user trees with complete vital dates are unrepresentative of the 1940 census population, skewing toward native-born, literate, and demographically stable households where transcription errors are least prevalent. Furthermore, if census names guided the initial profile attachment, evaluating name-based linkage against them is partially circular. Presenting these rates as 'Verified Correct' rather than agreement with a genealogical benchmark invites referee skepticism.

**How to check**  
Examine demographic characteristics (race, nativity, literacy, name commonness) of records in mlp_training1930_1940.dta relative to the full 1940 census sample; verify whether census names were used in constructing unique_ID_to_census_crosswalk1930.dta and 1940.dta.

**Suggested fix**  
Relabel 'Verified Correct' to 'Agrees with FamilySearch Reference' or 'Genealogical Match' in table rows and prose, and explicitly discuss sample selection and circularity risks in the paper's methodology section.
