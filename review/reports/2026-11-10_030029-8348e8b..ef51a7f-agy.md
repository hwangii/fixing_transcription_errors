## Findings

### 1. The genealogical reference standard is selected on prior successful census linkage and vital-record coverage, but is presented as independent validation

`selected-crosswalk-presented-as-independent-validation` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **likely** (implied, depends on unseen data)

**Where:** `CODE/create_linked_sample_for_MLP_training.do:5-13, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:104-108, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:573-580`

**What the code does**  
CODE/create_linked_sample_for_MLP_training.do:5-13 constructs mlp_training1930_1940.dta by taking pre-existing crosswalks (unique_ID_to_census_crosswalk1930.dta and unique_ID_to_census_crosswalk1940.dta), keeping 1:1 matches, and filtering on observed, ordered vital dates: keep if birthyr!=. & deathyr!=. & birthmon!=. & deathmon!=. & _merge==3 & ( birthyr < deathyr | (birthyr == deathyr & birthmo <= deathmo) ). The validation standard is therefore restricted to people already linked across both censuses within FamilySearch tree profiles who also have complete birth and death dates. The paper at lines 104-108 and 573-580 presents this as an 'independent set of high-quality genealogical links' from outside the census that 'distinguishes genuine recovery from spurious matching.'

**Why it matters**  
Genealogical profile linkage is not independent of name transcription quality: individuals with common, Anglicized, or easily readable names are far more likely to be found and attached to FamilySearch profiles, while unusual or misspelled names (the primary target of the ML model) are systematically under-represented. Furthermore, if the upstream FamilySearch profile-to-census crosswalks relied on transcribed census names (from Ancestry or FamilySearch), candidate links agreeing with those transcriptions are circular. Conditioning on complete vital dates additionally selects on nativity, socioeconomic status, and longevity. Presenting this selected sample as independent ground truth without selection diagnostics leaves the paper vulnerable to a standard referee objection in historical record linkage.

**How to check**  
Tabulate truth-set inclusion by name commonness, race, nativity, literacy, and baseline transcription disagreement (congruent == 0) using census data, and inspect the matching methodology of unique_ID_to_census_crosswalk1930/1940.dta to verify whether transcribed census names entered the profile linkage.

**Suggested fix**  
Document the construction of the upstream profile-to-census crosswalks and explicitly state whether census names were used in establishing profile links. Qualify the claim that the genealogical links provide an independent reference standard by acknowledging selection into FamilySearch trees and complete vital records, and provide selection diagnostics comparing the truth set to the broader census population.
