## Findings

### 1. Permitted fallback discrepancies can make the linkage runs differ for reasons other than target-only substitution

`fallback-tolerance-confounds-linkage-contrast` · **minor** · identification · confidence: **likely** (implied, depends on unseen data)

**Where:** `MATCHING/1c_tgt_divided_files.do:105-114, MATCHING/1c_tgt_divided_files.do:128-135, review/briefs/2026-11-17-tgt-check-nonfatal.md:3-5`

**What the code does**  
The code counts discrepancies with `name_true_cleaned_exists==0 & !(f_name==f_ans & l_name==l_ans)` but now exits only when ``nbad' > 0.001*`nchk'`. Thus the documented 436 discrepancies are allowed. For a discrepant non-target record, lines 111-114 put `_ans_cleaned` names into the target-only input, while the all-record baseline retains the differing `_true_cleaned` fallback, even though neither difference represents use of a model name.

**Why it matters**  
If any of the 436 records are non-targets, the intended comparison is not an exact target-only versus all-record model-name counterfactual. Different fallback cleaning can alter those records' links and, through uniqueness or candidate competition, potentially other links. The affected result may be negligible, but the current global 0.1% tolerance does not establish that.

**How to check**  
Before replacement, run `count if name_true_cleaned_exists==0 & !(f_name==f_ans & l_name==l_ans) & !tgt` and tabulate the discrepancies by `tgt`. After producing both link files, merge them by census ID and count changed link assignments involving these IDs or their competing candidates.

**Suggested fix**  
Reconcile the two fallback-cleaning paths or require zero discrepancies specifically among non-target records. If proceeding temporarily, report the consequential non-target count and show that excluding those records and affected candidate sets does not change the comparison.
