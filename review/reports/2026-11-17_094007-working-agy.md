### 2. Millions of records without measured target status are assigned to the non-target group

`unknown-target-status-coded-nontarget` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **likely** (implied, depends on unseen data)

**Where:** `MATCHING/1c_tgt_divided_files.do:17-19, MATCHING/1c_tgt_divided_files.do:54-65, MATCHING/1c_tgt_divided_files.do:100-114, review/briefs/2026-11-17-tgt-diag.md:3-7`

**What the code does**  
Target status is derived solely from `census1940_all_labeled_data.dta` via `tgt = !(namefrst==namefrst_fs & namelast==namelast_fs)`. Divided-file records unmatched to the target flag receive `tgt=0` at line 101, combining observed commercial agreements with 4,819,575 records absent from the labeled dataset (which excludes records with blank names or missing ages in either commercial source).

**Why it matters**  
For these 4.8 million records, commercial transcription agreement has not been confirmed. Coding them as non-targets forces Ancestry names rather than model names. Through candidate competition in the ABE matching algorithm, this affects match rates and link composition for both these records and others, creating potential selection bias in the purported target-only counterfactual.

**How to check**  
Examine the diagnostic output from tgt_fallback_diag.do for the age and birthplace distribution of the 4.8M unmatched records. Run linkage sensitivity checks treating this group under alternative rules (e.g. model names vs. Ancestry fallback) and evaluate changes in match rates and precision.

**Suggested fix**  
Explicitly document the handling of records absent from census1940_all_labeled_data.dta in the paper/slides. Perform a sensitivity check showing whether retaining Ancestry names versus applying model names to this group materially affects the resulting linkage distribution.
