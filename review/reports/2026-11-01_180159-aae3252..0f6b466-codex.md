## Findings

### 1. The headline claims improved transcription quality without any adjudicated name truth

`unsupported-transcription-quality-claims` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:66, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:122, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:279-305, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:797, CODE/compute_model_agreement_on_discordant.do:123-136`

**What the code does**  
The code counts agreement with the two commercial transcriptions, for example `namefrst==namefrst_ml & namelast==namelast_ml_ditto` and the analogous FamilySearch expression. It never compares the model output with a blinded, adjudicated reading of the census image. The draft itself concedes at lines 279-305 that these are resemblance measures and that no adjudicated comparison exists, but still says the method “improves name transcription,” “systematically improve[s] transcription quality,” and has demonstrated that conclusion.

**Why it matters**  
This is the paper's central claim, not a labeling detail. Agreement and improved linkage can arise from shared conventions or shared errors—especially the explicitly acknowledged FamilySearch/ditto asymmetry—so the available results do not establish that names are more accurate.

**How to check**  
Draw a fixed random sample stratified by commercial-transcription agreement and model-output status, blind adjudicators to source, independently transcribe the images, and tabulate exact-name and character error rates for Ancestry, FamilySearch, and the model.

**Suggested fix**  
Until an adjudicated sample exists, replace claims of improved transcription accuracy or quality throughout with the narrower verified claims: improved agreement with commercial transcriptions and improved linkage outcomes. Retain the explicit caveat already present at lines 279-305.

### 2. Aggregate middle-initial rates over different linked samples do not validate the additional links

`changing-linked-samples-do-not-identify-marginal-link-quality` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `CODE/create_figures_SSHA2025.do:72-83, CODE/create_figures_SSHA2025.do:203-211, LINKAGE/code/03_mortality/05_make_linkage_table.do:75-99, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:547-549, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:657-658`

**What the code does**  
The census analysis separately defines `validated_before` among `linked_before==1` and `validated_after` among `linked_after==1`, then compares their two conditional means. The mortality script similarly computes middle-initial agreement over every old-name link and every new-name link after separate `keep` operations. Neither calculation isolates links added by the new transcription or holds the linked population fixed, despite the mortality prose calling the result a check “on the additional links.”

**Why it matters**  
Improved transcription changes who enters the linked sample. An aggregate validation rate can rise because retained or newly selected records have different middle-initial availability or difficulty even if the marginal added links are poor. Thus the reported percentages are descriptive composition changes and do not by themselves support the claims that the new links are true rather than spurious.

**How to check**  
Merge old and new link files by source and target IDs, classify pairs as retained, added, removed, or changed, and tabulate middle-initial availability and agreement separately for each class. For changed links, compare the old and new proposed partners on the same source person.

**Suggested fix**  
Report validation separately for retained, added, removed, and changed links, including middle-initial availability in each group. Reword the existing aggregate comparisons as descriptive until the added-link results are available.

### 3. The reported 97–99% model-performance figures have no producing code in the package

`ml-performance-results-have-no-producing-code` · **MAJOR** · replication · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `replication/claims.toml:143-180, replication/check_claims.py:10-13, replication/check_claims.py:149-159, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:256-278, F:/github_repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:946-951`

**What the code does**  
The claim registry marks the first-name, last-name, prose, and appendix performance figures as `status = "nocode"` with empty expressions such as `values = ["", ""]`. The checker turns each empty expression into `UNCHECKED`, and its documented behavior deliberately does not fail the run for unchecked claims. Consequently, a successful claim-check run cannot reproduce or verify these figures.

**Why it matters**  
These are the paper's primary reported model-evaluation results. A replicator cannot recover the test split, normalization rules, sequence metric, character denominator, or the four percentages, and stale or differently defined figures would pass the current automated check.

**How to check**  
Run `rg -n 'table1-accuracy|appendix-accuracy|status = "nocode"' replication/claims.toml` and inspect `replication/claims_report.md`; the claims appear as unchecked rather than matched to generated results.

**Suggested fix**  
Archive the exact test-set construction and evaluation code, dependency versions, model checkpoint or deterministic inference outputs, and a generated result file containing all four raw metrics. Point `claims.toml` to those generated keys and make missing ML results fail the replication run.
