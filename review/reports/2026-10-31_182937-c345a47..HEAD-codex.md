## Findings

### 1. The conclusion claims reduced demographic and geographic bias even though the analysis estimates only linkage rates

`bias-reduction-not-estimated` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:\repos\6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb\DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:784, D:\repos\6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb\DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:797, CODE/compute_subgroup_linkage_rates.do:27-38`

**What the code does**  
For each selected subgroup, the code computes `summarize linked_before if cond`, `summarize linked_after if cond`, and the proportional change `100*(a/b-1)`. It does not compare newly linked people with unlinked people, estimate selection bias in an outcome, or measure geographic bias. The draft itself acknowledges at line 784 that whether estimates become less biased is not settled, but line 797 nevertheless says the method is “reducing demographic and geographic biases.”

**Why it matters**  
A linkage-rate increase does not establish that a linked sample is more representative or that estimates derived from it are less biased. Selection can persist or even change adversely depending on which records are newly linked, making the final claimed contribution stronger than the design supports.

**How to check**  
Tabulate characteristics and relevant downstream outcomes separately for always-linked, newly linked, and still-unlinked records, then compare each group with the full eligible population. No such decomposition is produced by the cited subgroup script.

**Suggested fix**  
Replace “while reducing demographic and geographic biases” with a claim limited to expanded linkage coverage, consistent with line 784. Retain a bias-reduction claim only after adding an explicit representativeness or estimand-bias analysis.
