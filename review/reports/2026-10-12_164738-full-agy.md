### 3. Model agreement with FamilySearch is interpreted as evidence of FamilySearch transcription reliability without ground truth

`familysearch-reliability-inferred-from-model-agreement` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:293-294, CODE/compute_model_agreement_on_discordant.do:119-124`

**What the code does**  
In CODE/compute_model_agreement_on_discordant.do:119-124, the code counts exact string equality between the model and FamilySearch (51.8%) versus Ancestry (14.3%) among discordant records. DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:293-294 asserts that this split suggests 'FamilySearch transcriptions may be somewhat more reliable on average.'

**Why it matters**  
Neither Ancestry nor FamilySearch is ground truth. A higher rate of agreement indicates that the model's output resembles FamilySearch's transcription more often, which can result from model training distributions, preprocessing, or shared ditto resolution rules rather than higher transcription accuracy. Conflating agreement with reliability violates the core domain distinction of the project.

**How to check**  
Inspect CODE/compute_model_agreement_on_discordant.do:119-124 to confirm that the script only tests string equality between sources without image review or external ground truth. Contrast with DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:293-294.

**Suggested fix**  
State descriptively that the model agrees more often with FamilySearch than Ancestry among discordant records. Remove the claim that this indicates FamilySearch transcriptions are more reliable on average unless supported by an image-adjudicated ground truth sample.

Review DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:179-180, 258-276, and 870-892. Search CODE/ for any script evaluating out-of-sample sequence/token accuracy.
