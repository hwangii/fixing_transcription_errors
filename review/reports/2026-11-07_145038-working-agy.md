### 3. FamilySearch name usage rates are reported only conditional on linkage, concealing baseline availability in the target population

`familysearch-exposure-conditioned-on-linkage` · **minor** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:90-93, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:120-121, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:173-184, MATCHING/1b_fs_divided_files.do:83-88`

**What the code does**  
In lines 90-93, `fs\`y\'_\`c\'' is generated with `if linked_\`c\'==1`, leaving unlinked records as missing (`.`). Lines 120-121 summarize `fs\`y\'_\`c\'`, so the exported mean reflects FamilySearch usage strictly among records that successfully linked. The table at lines 173-184 reports 'Links with an FS name in <year> (%)', but does not report the baseline availability of FamilySearch names in the candidate or target population.

**Why it matters**  
Because the FamilySearch specifications represent a hybrid rule falling back field by field to Ancestry when FamilySearch names are blank, differences in linkage rates reflect both transcription quality and selective substitution into the candidate pool. Conditioning exposure solely on successful linkage conceals whether differential linkage performance is driven by transcription quality or differential availability across subgroups.

**How to check**  
Compute the baseline share of records with non-empty FamilySearch names in the candidate divided files / 1940 labeled data, and compare it with the linked shares reported at lines 120-121.

**Suggested fix**  
Report the baseline FamilySearch availability/substitution rate in the candidate target and all-records populations as additional rows in the table alongside the existing conditional-on-linkage rows.
