### 1. The FamilySearch columns conceal selective fallback to Ancestry names and lack coverage reporting

`familysearch-columns-conceal-ancestry-fallback` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:81, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:145-160, MATCHING/1b_fs_divided_files.do:83-87, MATCHING/0b_create_fs_names.do:91-106`

**What the code does**  
In MATCHING/1b_fs_divided_files.do:83-87, FamilySearch names replace Ancestry names only where cleaned FamilySearch fields are non-empty, falling back to Ancestry independently for first and last names. In MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:81, the merge keeps only id_A mi1930 mi1940 fiveyr_band, dropping the fs_used indicators. The resulting table columns at lines 145-160 are labeled FS 1930 & 1940, FS 1930, and FS 1940 without reporting what share of records actually used FamilySearch transcriptions.

**Why it matters**  
FamilySearch coverageΓÇöparticularly in 1930 via final1930.dtaΓÇöis incomplete and selective. The linkage rates and validation shares reported for these columns reflect an Ancestry-FamilySearch hybrid rule rather than pure FamilySearch transcription performance. Without reporting coverage rates, readers cannot determine whether differences across columns or years reflect transcription quality or differing FamilySearch availability.

**How to check**  
In MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do, modify line 81 to keep fs_used1930 and fs_used1940 from the link files. Tabulate fs_used1930 and fs_used1940 overall and by target status. Compute linkage rates and validation shares separately for records with and without FamilySearch substitution.

**Suggested fix**  
Retain and display FamilySearch coverage (share of records with FamilySearch names used) for each column in the output table, or decompose performance by substitution status. Explicitly label the columns as FamilySearch with Ancestry fallback in table titles and notes.

### 2. Validation percentages are presented side by side while their changing sample sizes are suppressed

`validation-sample-sizes-suppressed` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:103-108, MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:147-165`

**What the code does**  
The script computes validated counts (nv_* and nva_*) alongside validation shares (sv_* and sva_*) at lines 103-108, but the TeX output loop at lines 147-165 writes only the percentages (lr, sv, sva). Unlike create_figures_SSHA2025.do, which reports # of records alongside Rate (Share), this table suppresses all sample sizes.

**Why it matters**  
Unlike the linkage rate denominator which is constant across methods, the share validated denominator is restricted to linked pairs with non-missing middle initials in both years. This denominator changes substantially across columns (e.g. from 308,513 in Ancestry to 618,732 in ML for the target group). Omitting sample sizes conceals major shifts in sample composition and prevents readers from evaluating the precision and coverage of the validation metric.

**How to check**  
Inspect MATCHING/results/fs_avg_performance.tex and lines 147-165 of MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do to verify that no record counts appear in the table. Check console output at lines 109-110 to see the variation in nv and nva across columns.

**Suggested fix**  
Add rows or columns for the number of validated records (N validated) and validation coverage (N validated / N linked) in MATCHING/results/fs_avg_performance.tex, following the exhibit format of tab:avg_performance.

The Share validated (%) row does not measure linkage quality against a uniform validator; it conflates matching accuracy with differences in initial extraction across sources. **How to check**

Trace lines 206-225 of MATCHING/3_append_and_clean_all_links_christian_torbenV2.do and lines 53-67 of MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do to confirm that mi in f_ans originates from matching_intgen whereas mianc1940 originates from divided_file.
