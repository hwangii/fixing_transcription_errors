# Review of new Section 4.4 "Comparison with a Second Human Transcription" — Claude, 2026-11-08

Source: Claude, reviewing the Claude Code handoff (`-prompt.md`) in-session, before the GPT and Gemini reports were seen. All prose numbers were checked against the three tabulars; all match. The 94% arithmetic in handoff items 3 and 4 checks (93.8% and 94.2%).

1. **The lede is buried: FamilySearch's 1940 names beat ours.** "Our transcriptions therefore deliver most of the gain…" is true (10.5 of 11.2 pp) but softens the fact that FS-1940 links 20.1% vs our 19.4% at the same validation. Say it plainly, then say why it matters: FamilySearch's census transcription is not distributed to researchers, so for anyone working from IPUMS the choice is Ancestry's names or ours, and ours recover most of what the unavailable second transcription would. Without that sentence a referee reads Table 9 as "so just use FamilySearch."

2. **Drop "the most informative cell is the first" and restructure the cells paragraph around the pair.** Cell 1 is defined by the model siding with Ancestry, so Ours ≈ Ancestry by construction; the content is that FS is worse there (13.0% / 77.5% fixed-initial). Cell 2 is the mirror: the model sides with FS and Ancestry is worse (6.7% / 53.0%). The real finding: whichever human transcription the model sides with is the one that links more records at higher validation, in both cells. That is direct evidence the model reads the image rather than one firm's conventions.

3. **Scope the cell-1 "arrives at the more useful one" sentence.** "More useful for linkage" (not "correct") is right, but add that in cell 1 our names add nothing over Ancestry's (21.4 vs 21.6), so the contribution is relative to the FS policy, not the baseline. Scoring FS links against Ancestry's initials is fair (same 1940 record's initial for every version); the own-initials 67.8 is the unfair number, so lead with 77.5.

4. **Cell 1 reveals a spillover the note must explain.** Ours 21.4% vs Ancestry 21.6% with identical names: names changed in cells 2–4 alter uniqueness for cell-1 records. Add to the Table 11 note that linkage is computed on the full file and a cell's rate under one version reflects names changed in every cell.

5. **Cell 3 is honest; make the consequence explicit.** FS-1940 links 14.1% vs our 8.9%, validates 68.4 vs 64.8 on fixed initials; on the records meant to show the model's independent contribution, the second human reading is substantially better. Add cell 4 (FS 15.0% vs ours 8.4%). Cells 3 and 4 are a third of the target set and are where the model loses to FS; this should feed back into the introduction's "character-level prediction avoids biasing toward common names" claim.

6. **Closing sentence is near-tautological in its first two clauses.** Replace with: "where our model sides with one human transcription, that is the transcription whose names link better; where it sides with neither, or returns no name, FamilySearch's names link roughly twice as many records as ours."

7. **"cannot yet match because we have not applied our model to the 1930 census" presumes the outcome.** "which would require applying our model to the 1930 census as well." Add a sentence on the All row: FS in 1930 alone raises the overall rate 22.8 → 24.3, nearly as much as our 1940 transcriptions (24.6) — the clearest case in the paper for a 1930 run.

8. **Table 9 All row under Ancestry initials is 86.0 → 86.0.** The full-sample validation gain in Table 4 (+1%) is entirely re-read initials. Section 4.1 (line 554) says "most of which"; change to "all of which" and cite Table 9. Line 549's "8 to 10 of its 18 points" is now pinned at 10.3 (65.4 → 75.7).

9. **Table 10 residual rows contradict the sample definition; investigate, don't footnote.** (a) 741 "human name field is empty" vs. "all four name fields present"; (b) 205,143 "raw strings agree" vs. target set defined by disagreement — cleaning should make more pairs agree, not fewer, so raw-agree/cleaned-disagree points to asymmetric cleaning (ditto resolution? middle-initial handling?) or an ID mismatch. "Cannot be matched to a transcribed record" sounds like cell 4; say why it isn't.

10. **Clarify "own transcription of the middle initials" for mixed versions** (FS-1940-only presumably = FS 1940 initial vs Ancestry 1930 initial).

11. **Cell 4 own-initials row (80.1% on 9,531) should be suppressed or greyed**, not just footnoted: 9% of links scored vs 25% under Ancestry initials.

12. **Add the replacement policy to Section 3.2** ("where our model produced no output for a field, Ancestry's is used for that field").

13. Minor: Table 9 caption → "Linkage under Ancestry's, our, and FamilySearch's transcriptions."

Overall: nothing wrong, numbers consistent; the main risk is tone — the section reads as defending the model against FamilySearch when the stronger story is the adjudication result (item 2) plus the public-availability argument (item 1).
