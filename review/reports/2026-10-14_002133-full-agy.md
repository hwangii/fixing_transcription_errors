### 1. The paper claims improved transcription quality although the code measures only agreement and linkage outcomes

`transcription-improvement-claimed-without-name-ground-truth` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:66-71, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:90-95, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:121-133, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:750-765, CODE/compute_model_agreement_on_discordant.do:116-130`

**What the code does**  
`CODE/compute_model_agreement_on_discordant.do:116-130` evaluates machine transcriptions strictly by exact agreement with commercial transcriptions: Ancestry (`namefrst==namefrst_ml & namelast==namelast_ml_ditto`) and FamilySearch (`fs_namefrst==namefrst_ml & fs_namelast==namelast_ml_ditto`). It does not compare machine output against any verified or adjudicated name ground truth. While Section 2.3 (lines 278-282, 302-304), Section 1 (lines 104-106), and Section 3.3 (lines 648-652) correctly acknowledge that agreement with commercial transcribers is resemblance rather than reliability, that link validation evaluates links rather than names, and that name validation against genealogical profiles is uncompleted, the Abstract (lines 66, 71), Introduction (lines 90, 121, 131-133), and Conclusion (lines 750, 765) repeatedly claim that the method 'improves name transcription', 'improve[s] handwriting recognition', 'systematically improve[s] transcription quality', and achieves 'dramatic improvements' in data construction.

**Why it matters**  
The central headline of the paper is improving transcription quality. But agreement with commercial transcriptions or increased downstream linkage does not prove that transcription accuracy or quality improved. If the model simply learns FamilySearch's spelling or ditto conventions, agreement and downstream matches rise without accuracy necessarily improving. A referee will challenge claims of improved transcription accuracy where only agreement and linkage are demonstrated.

**How to check**  
Run `rg -n -i "improv.*transcription|transcription quality|higher accuracy|accurate transcription" D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex` and compare each match with `CODE/compute_model_agreement_on_discordant.do:116-130`. Confirm that no script or exhibit evaluates machine output against an adjudicated ground truth of transcribed names.

**Suggested fix**  
Consistently describe the paper's demonstrated empirical results as improved census linkage outcomes, middle-initial consistency, and agreement with commercial transcriptions. Soften or qualify claims in the Abstract, Introduction, and Conclusion from 'improving name transcription' and 'improving transcription quality' to 'improving census linkage outcomes through machine learning transcription' until the profile-name validation described in Section 3.3 is completed.

### 4. The conclusion claims reduced empirical bias although the code estimates only subgroup linkage rates

`bias-reduction-claim-not-estimated` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:752-753, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:765, CODE/compute_subgroup_linkage_rates.do:20-28, CODE/compute_race_linkage_rates.do:26-38, CODE/create_figures_SSHA2025.do:265-330`

**What the code does**  
The subgroup analysis in `CODE/create_figures_SSHA2025.do:265-330`, `CODE/compute_subgroup_linkage_rates.do`, and `CODE/compute_race_linkage_rates.do` computes baseline and post-improvement linkage rates for demographic groups and calculates percentage increases (`100*(a/b - 1)`). The code does not compare the joint distribution of linked samples to the census population, nor does it estimate any empirical model (e.g., intergenerational mobility, wage returns) to assess bias reduction. In fact, for race, the linkage rate rose by 6.4% for non-whites (11.0% to 11.7%) and 7.9% for whites (24.1% to 26.0%), widening the absolute white/non-white gap from 13.1 pp to 14.3 pp. Yet the Conclusion asserts that results 'reduce biases in the empirical findings derived from them' (line 752) and 'reduc[e] demographic and geographic biases' (line 765).

**Why it matters**  
Higher linkage rates within subgroups expand sample sizes but do not demonstrate that sample selection bias or downstream parameter estimate bias has been reduced. Claiming that empirical bias is reduced without estimating representativeness or downstream models is an unsupported empirical claim that referees will reject.

**How to check**  
Search `CODE/` for any population representativeness weights, balance tests against the full 1940 population, or regressions of substantive outcomes. Inspect `CODE/compute_subgroup_linkage_rates.do` and `CODE/compute_race_linkage_rates.do` to verify that only subgroup means of `linked_before` and `linked_after` are calculated.

**Suggested fix**  
Revise lines 752-753 and 765 to state that machine learning transcription increases linkage rates and expands sample sizes for historically under-linked groups, while explicitly acknowledging that determining whether this reduces bias in specific downstream empirical estimates remains an open question.
