# Combined assessment — Section 4.4 "Comparison with a Second Human Transcription" (2026-11-08)

Three reviews of the Claude Code handoff: GPT (ChatGPT Pro), Gemini (4 Argon, high thinking), Claude. Claude recomputed every numerical claim in the GPT and Gemini reports against the three tabulars. Items marked **verified** reproduce from the tables; **check in code** means the tables cannot settle it.

## Consensus (all three)

1. **Drop "The most informative cell is the first."** Cell 1 is defined by the model agreeing with Ancestry, so Ours ≈ Ancestry by construction; present it as the model *selecting* Ancestry's reading, and note that its contribution is loss-avoidance relative to the FS policy (≈173–177k links), zero relative to the Ancestry baseline. Gemini adds a good robustness citation: with FS names in 1930 too, Ancestry-1940 still beats FS-1940 by the same margin (22.8 vs 14.2), so the result is not an Ancestry-1930 house-style artifact. **Verified.**
2. **Explain why identical names give different rates** (cell 1: 21.6 vs 21.4; cell 2: 25.9 vs 25.7). Likely full-file uniqueness spillover from names changed in other cells; GPT adds that the note must say whether Table 11 stratifies the full runs or re-links within cells (only the former is an additive decomposition of Table 9). **Check in code, then state.**
3. **Cell 4 must report FS-1940's 15.0%** and say that the available model field is retained (partial fallback), which is why Ours rises 6.9 → 8.4. Gemini shows cell 4 alone accounts for ~80% of the net gap between Ours (19.4) and FS-1940 (20.1): cell 1 (+173k) nearly offsets cell 3 (−180k), leaving cell 4 (−83k) and the residual (−26k). **Verified from the tables, ±rounding.**
4. **Rewrite the closing sentence**: tautological in its first two clauses, omits cell 4, and "better/worse" should refer to linkage outcomes relative to FS-1940 substitution, not transcription fidelity. All three propose essentially the same replacement (the model selects the human reading that links better; where it departs from both or abstains, it trails FS).
5. **Soften "at the same share validated"** to "similar," and say "94 percent of the net increase over Ancestry" rather than "most of the gain." **Verified.**
6. **Drop "because we have not applied our model to the 1930 census"** as a causal claim; state only that the model-in-both-years comparison does not exist.
7. **Reconcile the Table 10 residual rows** with the sample definition (741 empty-field records inside an all-fields-present sample; 205,143 raw-agree records inside a disagreement-defined target set). Gemini's reconstruction: on the 205,884 excluded records Ours has 2,838 Ancestry-initial links but only 27 own-initial links, so they effectively fell back to Ancestry — they behave like cell 4. **Verified arithmetic (620,414 − 617,576; 618,732 − 618,705).** Investigate the cleaned-vs-raw mismatch rather than footnote it.
8. **Define the initial sources for every version** in the Table 9 note (Ours = model 1940 initial + Ancestry 1930 initial; FS-1940-only = FS 1940 + Ancestry 1930, etc.), and state that each percentage is over that version's links with both initials observed.

## Raised by two

9. **"The gap persists" under Ancestry initials is selective** (GPT, Gemini). In cell 1 the Ours–FS gap shrinks from 15.2 to 5.5 pp; in cell 3 the FS–Ours gap shrinks from 12.2 to 3.6 pp. Gemini's mechanism is worth stating: a transcription that misread the name usually misread the initial too, so own-initial scoring penalizes whichever source is wrong in that cell — which is also why both Ours and FS drop 11.4 pp in cell 2 under Ancestry's (wrong) initials. **Verified.**
10. **Links lost outside the target set** (GPT, Gemini; also in the 2026-11-02 combined report §2.2). Table 9 implies Ours loses ≈595k links on the 36.6M concordant records (28.27% → 26.65%), while FS-1940 substitution loses only ≈0.5 pp (→ 27.76%) there. Both policies change names only on the target set, so pure collision effects should be of similar size; Ours causes three times the collateral loss. Gemini concludes the model must be applied to concordant records too; GPT says verify. **The handoff says replacement is target-set only, so this is the single most important thing to check in code.** If the model is only applied to the target set, the extra loss needs another explanation (e.g. first-name formatting changing cleaned keys, or the 10% no-output handling).
11. **The 24% "neither" cell is not shown to add links unavailable from either human source** (GPT; Claude on the framing). Marginal rates cannot say whether the ~308k model links in cell 3 overlap the ~489k FS-1940 links. GPT's fix is cheap and directly answers the referee: from the existing link files, count model links absent from the union of the Ancestry and FS-1940 link sets, separating newly linked 1940 people from changed 1930 partners. Recommended.
12. **Cell 3 "1930 record may carry an error of its own"**: Gemini says drop (FS-1940 links 14.1% of the same records to the same 1930 file, and switching 1930 to FS barely moves cell 3, so the 1930 side is not what blocks the ~180k records FS links and we don't); GPT says keep as a limitation. Claude sides with Gemini on substance: keep at most a clause, immediately followed by the FS comparison that shows a linkable reading exists for many of these records.

## Raised by one

13. **Public-availability framing** (Claude). FS's transcription is proprietary; the section should say the comparison is one only the authors can run, and that for IPUMS users the choice is Ancestry's names or ours. Without it, Table 9 reads as "use FamilySearch." Neither GPT nor Gemini raised this; it is paper-level framing rather than a section defect, but it determines how the section lands.
14. **Table 9 All row, Ancestry initials: 86.0 → 86.0** (Claude). The full-sample validation gain in Table 4 is entirely re-read initials; line 554 should say "all," not "most."
15. **"percent of their links are validated" misstates the denominator** (GPT): ~22% of links carry both initials. State the event and denominator at first use.
16. **Within-cell levels are not comparable across cells** (Gemini, GPT): cells 3–4 reach only 15.6–16.4% even with FS in both years, vs 28.7% in cell 2, so the partition tracks legibility as well as which source erred. Add one sentence when introducing Table 11.
17. **Cell 4 own-initials row rests on a selected 9% of links** (Claude, Gemini): suppress or grey it, and explain that when the model returns no first name there is no model initial.
