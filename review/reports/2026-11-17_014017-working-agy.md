### 1. Target-only flag in 1c_tgt_divided_files.do excludes discordant records with blank human fields and conflicting duplicate flags

**What the code does**  
MATCHING/1c_tgt_divided_files.do:53 sets tgt = namefrst!="" & namelast!="" & fs_namefrst!="" & fs_namelast!="" & !(namefrst==fs_namefrst & namelast==fs_namelast), and line 64 sets replace tgt = 0 if _conf for any histid with discordant flags across state rows. In contrast, the paper's target definition in MATCHING/fs_avg_performance.do:80 is !(namefrst==namefrst_fs & namelast==namelast_fs) from census1940_all_labeled_data.dta. In MATCHING/fs_cells.do:73-82 and fs_cells.tex, records where a human field is empty (cell 5, 741 records) and unclassified/conflicting records (cell 0, 205,143 records) are part of the paper's 14,416,543 target universe.

**Why it matters**  
More than 205,800 records that the paper classifies and evaluates as targets in its main exhibits are assigned tgt = 0 and thus retain Ancestry's transcriptions rather than model transcriptions. As a result, the target-only linkage file does not link on the paper's target universe, creating an unacknowledged sample discrepancy against published linkage benchmarks.

**How to check**

**Suggested fix**

### 3. Target-only substitution introduces asymmetric selection into the linkage sample between target and non-target records

`target-only-linkage-selection-asymmetry` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/1c_tgt_divided_files.do:122-125, MATCHING/_tgt_only_run.do:3-9, review/briefs/2026-11-17-target-only-links.md:3`

**What the code does**  
MATCHING/1c_tgt_divided_files.do:122-125 replaces 1940 names with model transcriptions only for records flagged as targets (Ancestry and FamilySearch disagree), while retaining Ancestry transcriptions for all non-target records.

**Why it matters**  
Conditioning name substitution on disagreement between commercial transcribers selectively treats records with difficult handwriting or rare names, while freezing concordant records at Ancestry's transcription even if both commercial transcribers made a shared transcription error. When linked against 1930 Ancestry names, changes in linkage rates conflate improved transcription accuracy on difficult records with sample composition effects. Furthermore, if Ancestry made identical transcription errors in both 1930 and 1940 on a target record, correcting only the 1940 name will break a link that previously matched on correlated error.

**How to check**  
Tabulate linkage rates and validation rates across the two new link files (mltgt1940 vs ml1940) separately for target records (tgt == 1) and non-target records (tgt == 0). Assess whether link gains on target records are accompanied by link losses or changes in composition.

**Suggested fix**  
In exhibits or slides comparing target-only links with baseline links, present linkage rates and validation rates decomposed by target status (tgt == 1 vs tgt == 0) and discuss the potential selection and correlated-error mechanisms explicitly.
