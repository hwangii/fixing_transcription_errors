## Findings

### 1. `CHECK PASSED` ignores the sample mismatches reported immediately above it

`pass-ignores-input-universe-mismatches` · **MAJOR** · correctness · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `MATCHING/check_mlp_abe_same_input.do:49-58, MATCHING/check_mlp_abe_same_input.do:69-77`

**What the code does**  
The script counts matched ABE records with `sex!=1`, MLP men absent from ABE, and ABE records absent from MLP at lines 51-56, but stores none of those counts. It then executes `keep if _merge==3`, retaining matched nonmen, and declares success solely when ``ndiff'==0 & `nboth'>0``. Thus sample parity and male status do not enter the pass condition, and `nboth` includes every matched record rather than only men.

**Why it matters**  
The check can certify identical male inputs even when the two linkage methods receive different sets of men, or even when its nonempty intersection contains only records not coded male in MLP. Any resulting ABE-versus-MLP difference could then reflect input composition rather than linkage method. Whether this affects the current files depends on the unseen mismatch counts.

**How to check**  
Run `do MATCHING/check_mlp_abe_same_input.do` and compare the three displayed mismatch counts with the final message. At the merge point, test `assert sex==1 if _merge==3`, `assert _merge==3 if sex==1`, and `assert _merge!=2`; any failure demonstrates that the present success condition can give false assurance.

**Suggested fix**  
Save all three mismatch counts in locals, restrict the field comparison to `_merge==3 & sex==1`, and require every mismatch local to equal zero in addition to `ndiff==0` and a positive male intersection before printing `CHECK PASSED`. Otherwise print failure and exit nonzero.
