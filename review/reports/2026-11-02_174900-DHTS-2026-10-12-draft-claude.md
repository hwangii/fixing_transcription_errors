## Major points

1. **The missing baseline: FamilySearch's own transcription.** The model agrees with FamilySearch in 52% of discordant cases and with Ancestry in 14%, and the "before" links use Ancestry only. How much of the 119% gain would come from substituting the FamilySearch name on discordant records, or linking on either name? Without that column the paper cannot separate "machine learning improves transcription" from "Ancestry's transcription of hard records is worse than FamilySearch's, and the model reproduces FamilySearch." Cheap to run; should be Table 4's third column.

2. **Independence of the genealogical reference standard.** The truth set is built from FamilySearch profiles, attached to 1940 records through FamilySearch's own index, i.e. its transcription. Given point 1, a link "the genealogical data confirm" may partly confirm agreement with FamilySearch's reading. Line 573 calls this "evidence from outside the census"; a referee will ask whether it is outside the FamilySearch transcription. Needs a paragraph and a check (does the verified-correct gain survive on records where the model disagrees with FamilySearch?).

3. **Panel B of Tables 5–8 is never discussed, and cuts both ways.** Added links on discordant ABE are 47% verified / 2.5% contradicted — excellent. But the new transcriptions also remove 147,928 verified-correct links (33% of removals); under HLINK full sample, 633,350 verified-correct links are lost against 1,135,269 gained. Report the churn and the net.

4. **Out-of-sample generalization is unreported.** Trained on Rhode Island plus "selected regions of other states" (line 184), applied to 49 jurisdictions; accuracy reported only on a 10% holdout of the same pool. Report Table 1 and the 66/14/52/24/10 breakdown by in-training vs. out-of-training states; say whether the model was retrained after expansion.

5. **The 10% "no usable transcription" cases (line 306).** 3.4 million discordant records get nothing from the pipeline. What name enters the linker for them? Never stated.

6. **Stale and forward-looking passages.** Section 4.4 (27 lines on an unrun exercise, "results are forthcoming"); conclusion line 790 says 1940 processing is still being scaled up; compute paragraph (371–379) extrapolates 730,000 GPU-hours with a TODO noting the realized cost exists. 1,600 GPU-hours for 300k records is ~19 GPU-seconds per record; say what it includes.

7. **Validation section structure undersells it.** Intro lists genealogical validation as the third main result, but it lives inside "Robustness to the Linking Algorithm" via "these tables also serve as our external validation" (line 573). Split into robustness and validation subsections; define *recall*.

8. **Why 52% FamilySearch vs 14% Ancestry?** Line 304 lists three possible reasons and leaves it. "Overlap between our training data and FamilySearch's transcriptions" is odd since training data is the consensus by construction. Rule the mechanical explanations (ditto resolution, normalization) in or out.
