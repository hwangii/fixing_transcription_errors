### 2. Middle-initial agreement with commercial transcriptions is treated as evidence of true matches

`middle-initial-agreement-treated-as-true-match-evidence` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:551-553, replication/diagnostics/validation_fixed_initials.do:76-77`

**What the code does**  
In replication/diagnostics/validation_fixed_initials.do:76-77, validation indicators vb_src and va_src measure whether a linked person's 1930 middle initial agrees with the single character in Ancestry's or FamilySearch's 1940 record. Line 552 of DHTS-2026-10-12.tex quotes the resulting 82 to 85 percent agreement rate on added links (versus 71 percent on dropped links), and line 553 concludes that this 'suggests that our improved transcriptions are recovering true matches rather than creating spurious ones.'

**Why it matters**  
Neither Ancestry nor FamilySearch provides ground truth; both are noisy commercial transcriptions. Single-letter middle initials agree by chance with substantial probability, and agreement between two machine/commercial readings can reflect shared transcription artifacts rather than correct links. Equating commercial middle-initial agreement with match 'quality' and inferring true matches overstates the diagnostic evidence and violates the project rule that agreement is not accuracy.

**How to check**  
Merge added (status==3) and dropped (status==4) links with the independent genealogical reference standard used in Section 5 (DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:565-585) and check whether middle-initial agreement with Ancestry or FamilySearch actually correlates with independent genealogical verification.

**Suggested fix**  
Describe these figures strictly as middle-initial agreement with comparison transcriptions rather than proof of true matches. Base claims about recovering true matches on the independent genealogical validation in Section 5.

### 3. Added-versus-dropped link comparison omits roughly 80 percent of links without disclosing missing-initial selection

`added-link-validation-covers-only-one-fifth` · **MAJOR** · identification · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:551-552, replication/diagnostics/validation_fixed_initials.do:95-106, replication/results/validation_fixed_initials.tex:44-49, replication/results/validation_fixed_initials.tex:84-89`

**What the code does**  
In replication/diagnostics/validation_fixed_initials.do:95-106, agreement means for each link status are calculated solely over records with non-empty middle initials in both censuses. For discordant records, usable initials exist for only 20.5% (Ancestry) and 20.8% (FamilySearch) of added links (pctMiAdded*Target), and 19.2% and 18.6% of dropped links. In DHTS-2026-10-12.tex:551-552, the text states that 'links that only our transcriptions produce agree in 82 to 85 percent of cases, against 71 percent for the links they replace', without disclosing that 80 percent of cases have no usable initials.

**Why it matters**  
Presenting the rate as holding 'in 82 to 85 percent of cases' invites the reader to believe it characterizes all added links. Middle initial recording in historical censuses is heavily selectiveΓÇödisproportionately absent among foreign-born, minority, and less-educated individuals, precisely the demographic groups the paper targets. Generalizing validation results from an unrepresentative one-fifth subsample to all recovered links without disclosure creates unaddressed selection bias.

**How to check**  
In Stata on temp.dta, run 'tab status if congruent==0 & !missing(va_anc)' and compare the race, nativity, literacy, and legibility distribution of added links with usable initials against added links without initials ('congruent==0 & status==3 & missing(va_anc)').

**Suggested fix**  
Disclose in the text at line 551 that the 82-85% and 71% rates apply only to the ~20% of links with usable middle initials in both censuses (matching the convention in Section 6, line 660, which explicitly notes 'among links with a middle initial recorded in both sources, about a quarter of all links').
