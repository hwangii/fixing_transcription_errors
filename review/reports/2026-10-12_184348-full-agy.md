### 1. Claim that directly contradicted links fall across the board is refuted by HLINK and by discordant-sample ABE

`directly-contradicted-links-claim-contradicted-by-hlink-and-abe` · **BLOCKER** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:104-106, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:559-561, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/figures/deaglan_abe_jw.tex:17, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/figures/deaglan_abe_jw_restricted.tex:17, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/figures/deaglan_hlink.tex:17, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/figures/deaglan_hlink_restricted.tex:17`

**What the code does**  
The paper claims in lines 104-106 and 560-561 that 'across the full sample the number of links the same data directly contradict falls from 190,805 to 171,174. Our transcriptions thus recover links that independent evidence confirms, rather than manufacturing matches that it rejects; Section 3.2 reports this for three linking algorithms.' In fact, under HLINK in the full sample (Table 6, figures/deaglan_hlink.tex:17), directly contradicted links increase from 293,078 to 296,657 (+3,579). Furthermore, on the target discordant sample (Tables 5 and 7), directly contradicted links increase under both ABE (from 40,919 to 43,715, +2,796) and HLINK (from 67,636 to 70,375, +2,739). The text selectively quotes the full-sample Jaro-Winkler reduction alongside discordant ABE verified gains, concealing increases in contradicted links across two of the three algorithms.

**Why it matters**  
This directly contradicts the paper's central external validation claim that improved transcriptions do not create demonstrably wrong matches across the three evaluated linkers. A referee will immediately see that directly contradicted links rise in three of the five reported settings.

**How to check**  
Inspect line 17 of figures/deaglan_abe_jw.tex, figures/deaglan_abe_jw_restricted.tex, figures/deaglan_hlink.tex, and figures/deaglan_hlink_restricted.tex. Compare the Old vs. New values of 'Directly Contradicted' across all three algorithms for both the full and restricted samples.

**Suggested fix**  
Revise lines 104-106 and 559-561 to accurately report the behavior of directly contradicted links across each algorithm and sample. Disclose that while Jaro-Winkler and full-sample ABE see declines, contradicted links increase under HLINK in the full sample (+3,579) and under both ABE (+2,796) and HLINK (+2,739) on the discordant target set. Emphasize that the verified correct link gains exceed the increase in contradicted links by roughly two orders of magnitude.

### 2. Table 2's identical 28.3% disagreement rate between US population and analysis sample is a Stata missing-value artifact, not an empirical finding

`us-column-incongruent-rate-artefact` · **MAJOR** · claims-vs-code · confidence: **verified** (traced in source)

**Where:** `CODE/munir_figures_for_nber.do:257, CODE/munir_figures_for_nber.do:326, CODE/munir_figures_for_nber.do:340, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/figures/balance_allstates.tex:5, D:/repos/6638fa0fd89fbec05130caeb/DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:433-435`

**What the code does**  
In CODE/munir_figures_for_nber.do:257, incongruent is defined conditionally: `gen incongruent=!(namefrst==namefrst_fs & namelast==namelast_fs) if linked==1 & namefrst!="" & namefrst_fs!="" & namelast!="" & namelast_fs!=""`. For records not in the analysis sample, incongruent is missing (`.`). Line 340 runs `balancetable (mean) (mean if in_analysis_sample==1) incongruent ...`. Stata's `(mean)` command silently ignores missing values, so the US population column calculates the mean of incongruent over the exact same 51,066,203 records as the Analysis Sample column.

**Why it matters**  
The paper claims at lines 434-435: 'The rate of transcription disagreement is 28.3 percent in both columns.' This is presented as substantive proof that the analysis sample is representative of the US population in its transcription disagreement. In fact, disagreement is undefined for records outside the analysis sample, and the numerical identity is a pure artifact of Stata dropping missings.

**How to check**  
In Stata, run `count if age>=10 & female==0`, `count if age>=10 & female==0 & !missing(incongruent)`, and `sum incongruent if age>=10 & female==0`. Notice r(N) for incongruent is 51,066,203 rather than 55,350,482, proving that column 1 dropped all unlinked records before taking the mean.

**Suggested fix**  
In balancetable and Table 2, omit incongruent from the US population column (or mark it as not applicable), and revise line 434 of DHTS-2024-11-18.tex to explain that transcription disagreement cannot be defined for the full US population because dual transcriptions do not exist for unlinked records.

## Checked and sound

- **The discordant-record agreement categories use a common human-complete denominator and form an asserted partition** — CODE/compute_model_agreement_on_discordant.do:109-137 uses a common human-complete denominator (all four human fields present), does not condition on model success, categorizes missing model output separately, and asserts that the four categories sum exactly to the denominator.
- **The paper avoids claiming FamilySearch is inherently more accurate than Ancestry** — DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:298-305 frames the 14% vs 52% split as resemblance rather than reliability, explicitly pointing to ditto-resolution conventions and normalizations rather than accuracy differences.
- **Headline linking table is generated directly by code and accounts for sample differences** — CODE/create_figures_SSHA2025.do:129-214 computes the rates directly and exports figures/avg_performance_allstates.tex, and DHTS-2024-11-18.tex:528-530 explicitly notes the small sample differences with Table 2.
